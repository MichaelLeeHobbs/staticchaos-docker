# Fist — Player Guide
> Train your body, build your Ki, and unleash devastating martial-arts combos with your bare hands.

## Overview

The Fist is the martial artist of Static Chaos — a pure melee fighter who needs no weapon to be deadly. Where other classes lean on spells or gear, the Fist channels **Ki** (inner energy) earned blow by blow, then spends it on a ladder of signature combo attacks ranging from a simple toe-stomp all the way to the legendary Touch of Death.

Your prime attribute is **Strength**, and your class is built around the highest **Spirit** of any class in the game (100), with very strong **Body** (90) too. Those two stats drive almost everything you do: Spirit powers your Ki techniques and auras, and Body fuels the raw damage of your strikes.

A Fist does **not** use mana. You are a hands-on bruiser who wins by staying in the fight, feeding your Ki meter, and timing the right combo at the right moment.

## How your power works

Everything important about a Fist is **trained**, not found on the ground. Gear gives you the usual hit points, AC, hitroll, damroll, and saves — but your actual martial power comes entirely from training.

There are three things you build up:

- **Basic attacks (mastery).** You learn six basic strikes in order — **shinkick, jab, spinkick, knee, elbow, uppercut** — each one mastered with experience. Every basic attack you throw both deals damage and has a chance to build your Ki.
- **Ki.** Each attack feeds a Ki meter that climbs as you fight. The higher your Ki, the bigger the combo you can release. But Ki is volatile — push it too high and it collapses to nothing, so there's real risk in stacking it up.
- **Discipline & body.** Spending experience raises your **discipline ranks** (which unlock special abilities) and **trains individual body parts** (torso, hands, arms, legs) for more power and toughness.

The heart of the class is the **combo**: when your Ki is high enough, the `combo` command fires off whatever technique matches your *current* Ki level. Low Ki gives you modest tricks; high Ki unleashes the truly nasty stuff. Releasing a combo spends your Ki, so you're constantly cycling between building energy and cashing it in.

## Core commands

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `master` | Your training hub. Type `master list` to see which basic attacks you've learned and how much Ki each one builds. Used to learn new attacks, raise your maximum Ki, and rearrange your training. |
| `shinkick` | Basic strike. Damages your target and may build Ki. |
| `jab` | Basic strike. Damages your target and may build Ki. |
| `spinkick` | Basic strike. Damages your target and may build Ki. |
| `knee` | Basic strike. Damages your target and may build Ki. |
| `elbow` | Basic strike. Damages your target and may build Ki. |
| `uppercut` | Basic strike. Damages your target and may build Ki. |
| `combo` | Release a combo technique based on your current Ki level. The higher your Ki, the more powerful the technique. Spends your Ki and requires you to be in combat. |
| `discipline` | Spend experience to advance to the next rank of Fist discipline (up to rank 10). Discipline ranks unlock the special abilities below. |
| `bodytrain` | Train a body part — `torso`, `hands`, `arms`, or `legs` — to grow stronger and tougher. |
| `eyesight` | (Discipline 1+) Sharpen your vision to see things you'd otherwise miss. Toggle on/off. |
| `levitate` | (Discipline 4+) Focus your Ki to lift yourself off the ground. Costs movement. |
| `roundhouse` | (Discipline 5+) A spinning kick that strikes everyone hostile in the room. Costs movement. |
| `innerfire` | (Discipline 7+) Turn your focus inward, spending Ki to burn away impurities affecting you. |
| `phoenixaura` | (Discipline 8+) Expand a spiritual aura that boosts your hitroll and damroll. Toggle on/off; costs movement to raise. |
| `dim-mak` | (Discipline 9+) A dark poisoning strike to the throat. Hits harder the more player-kills you've earned, and can disrupt enemy spellcasters. Costs movement (and optionally Ki). |

## Getting started

1. **Roll a Fist and learn your first attacks.** Type `master list` to see your training sheet. You begin by mastering the basic strikes in order, starting with **shinkick**. Each new attack costs experience, so spend kills wisely.
2. **Get into a fight and use your strikes.** In combat, cycle through the basic attacks you've learned (`shinkick`, `jab`, etc.). Each one chips at the enemy and slowly charges your Ki — watch for the message that your body begins to glow.
3. **Watch your Ki climb.** As you fight, you'll get messages as your Ki burns "brightly," then "with intense energy." This is your combo fuel building up.
4. **Fire a combo.** Once your Ki is high enough, type `combo` to release a technique. The exact move depends on how much Ki you've stored — early on you'll only reach the lower-tier techniques, but every point of maximum Ki you train opens up bigger ones.
5. **Grow your foundation.** Between fights, spend experience on `discipline` (to unlock abilities like roundhouse and phoenixaura), `bodytrain` (for raw power and durability), and raising your **maximum Ki** through `master`. This is the long game that turns a brawler into a master.

## Strengths & weaknesses

**Strengths**
- **No weapon required** — your fists are your weapon, and you can't be disarmed of them.
- **No mana dependency** — you never run dry on spell points; your fuel is Ki, earned by fighting.
- **Scaling burst damage** — a fully charged combo is brutal, and high-tier techniques hit extremely hard.
- **Highest Spirit in the game (100)** — your auras, Ki techniques, and special abilities all benefit.
- **Strong utility** — true sight, levitation, an area-effect roundhouse, a self-cleansing inner fire, and a stat-boosting Phoenix Aura.
- **Anti-defense tools** — high-Ki combos can shatter enemy defensive effects, and dim-mak can strip a caster's spell focus.

**Weaknesses**
- **Volatile Ki** — overcharging your Ki makes it collapse to zero. Mismanaging it wastes your turn and your fuel.
- **Combo gambles** — some Ki levels produce a *miss* (you stumble or hesitate) rather than a hit, so the combo ladder isn't all upside.
- **Stamina-hungry** — the strongest combos and discipline abilities (roundhouse, phoenixaura, dim-mak, levitate) eat movement points; an exhausted Fist can't perform them.
- **Slow to build** — your real power comes from a lot of experience spent on training, discipline, and maximum Ki. Early on you're a modest brawler.

## Tips

- **Type `master list` often.** It shows exactly which attacks you've learned, how much Ki each one feeds you, and your current maximum Ki. It's your dashboard.
- **Raise your maximum Ki.** A higher Ki ceiling lets you reach the more powerful combos. It's expensive but it's the difference between a toe-stomp and a Touch of Death.
- **Don't overcharge.** Pushing your Ki past your trained limit causes it to flare and collapse — you lose all of it. Release your combo before you blow past your ceiling.
- **Keep your movement topped up.** Roundhouse, Phoenix Aura, levitate, and dim-mak all spend stamina. Rest or refresh before a big fight so you can actually use your toolkit.
- **Run Phoenix Aura in tough fights.** It boosts your hitroll and damroll while active — just remember it costs movement to raise and turns off when you toggle it again.
- **Train all four body parts.** Torso, hands, arms, and legs each contribute; don't neglect any one of them.
- **Climb discipline steadily.** Each rank unlocks something useful, and many of your best tools (roundhouse at 5, phoenixaura at 8, dim-mak at 9) are gated behind it.

## Gear

For a Fist, gear is your **support layer, not your power source.** Equipment in Static Chaos only ever provides hit points, mana, movement, AC, hitroll, damroll, and saving throws — it does **not** grant or improve any of your martial techniques. Your strikes, combos, Ki, discipline abilities, and auras all come from **training**, never from an item.

What that means in practice:
- **Prioritize survivability and offense stats.** Look for gear with strong HP, AC, hitroll, and damroll so you can stay in melee and land your strikes. Extra **movement** is valuable too, since so many Fist abilities cost stamina.
- **You don't need a weapon.** Your hands are your weapon — focus your slots elsewhere.
- **Mana on gear is wasted on you.** You're not a spellcaster, so don't chase mana items.
- **No item is a substitute for training.** A perfectly geared Fist who hasn't trained discipline, body, and maximum Ki is still a beginner. Spend your experience — that's where your real strength lives. See the **Gearing Guide** for best-in-slot stats.
